Why does n_hrp2_positive start at 0 while light microscopy (LM) prevalence doesn't?
=====================================================================================

Short answer
------------
n_hrp2_positive starts at 0 because HRP2 status is tracked by a variable
(hrp2_infection_time) that only ever gets set when someone passes through the
"new infection" code path during the simulation. LM prevalence, by contrast,
is computed directly from each individual's disease state (S/A/D/U/Tr) at the
current timestep. set_equilibrium() seeds that state distribution to already
match steady-state prevalence for the configured EIR *before the simulation
starts* - but it does NOT seed hrp2_infection_time to match. So at t=0 there
is a population of already-infected people (per equilibrium) that LM correctly
counts as prevalent, but that the model has no record of ever "newly infecting",
so none of them are marked HRP2 positive.

The mechanism, step by step
----------------------------
1. create_variables() (R/variables.R) initialises two independent things:

   - `state` (R/variables.R:128): a CategoricalVariable set from
     initial_state()/initial_state_vivax(), which draws from the
     malariaEquilibrium(Vivax) solution for the configured init_EIR. This is
     why, even at timestep 0, a realistic fraction of the population already
     sits in A, D, U or Tr - confirmed empirically: with human_population =
     2000 and init_EIR = 10, t=0 already has S=782, A=909, D=12, U=297, Tr=0.

   - `hrp2_infection_time` (R/variables.R:293): an IntegerVariable, always
     initialised to -1 (the "not currently HRP2 positive" sentinel) for
     *every* individual, completely independently of what `state` was just
     initialised to. Confirmed empirically: right after create_variables(),
     100% of individuals have hrp2_infection_time == -1, including the ~909
     people equilibrium just placed in state A and the 12 placed in D.

2. HRP2 positivity is only switched on inside update_hrp2_and_render_infections()
   (R/hrp2.R), which is called from falciparum_infection_outcome_process() /
   vivax_infection_outcome_process() (R/human_infection.R) at the moment a
   bite produces a *new* clinical (to_D/treated) or new asymptomatic (to_A)
   infection. It sets hrp2_infection_time = timestep for exactly those
   individuals.

3. The people equilibrium initialised directly into A/D/Tr at t=0 never pass
   through that code path - they didn't get there via a bite resolved during
   the simulation, they simply started there as an initial condition. So none
   of them ever get an hrp2_infection_time seeded, and n_hrp2_positive
   (R/hrp2.R: create_hrp2_renderer_process, counts hrp2_infection_time != -1)
   is 0 at t=0 even though the population is already at realistic malaria
   prevalence per LM.

4. LM prevalence has no equivalent gap. create_prevalence_renderer()
   (R/render.R) computes n_detect_lm_* directly from `state$get_index_of(...)`
   at the current timestep - it has no memory or historical dependency, so it
   is immediately consistent with whatever `state` was initialised to,
   equilibrium included.

Why the shapes converge over time
----------------------------------
As the simulation runs, new infections occur every timestep (via bites), which
gradually populate hrp2_infection_time for a growing share of the population.
Combined with the hrp2_scale clearance hazard, the inflow of newly
(re)started HRP2-positive individuals eventually balances the outflow from
clearance, and n_hrp2_positive settles into its own steady state - at which
point its shape does track LM prevalence reasonably well (as seen in
scratch_hrp2.R's later timesteps). The mismatch is really a burn-in/initial-
condition artifact concentrated in roughly the first few multiples of
hrp2_scale timesteps, not a difference in ongoing dynamics.

What can be done about it
---------------------------
1. Treat it as a burn-in period: discard the first ~3-6 * hrp2_scale
   timesteps before comparing n_hrp2_positive to LM prevalence (this is
   already effectively what scratch_hrp2.R's later-timestep summaries do).
   No code change required.

2. Fix the root cause properly: seed hrp2_infection_time at initialisation
   for anyone equilibrium places directly into D, A or Tr (not S or U),
   assigning each a plausible negative "time since infection" instead of -1,
   so t=0 already reflects a realistic HRP2-positive population consistent
   with the equilibrium disease-state distribution. This is more involved -
   it requires deciding what distribution of "time since infection" to draw
   from for people who are already infected at t=0 (e.g. sampling consistent
   with the stationary age-since-infection distribution implied by the
   incidence rate and the hrp2_scale survival curve), rather than just
   picking an arbitrary constant offset.

I haven't made a code change for this - let me know if you'd like option 2
implemented.

Why is the LM/HRP2 delta so much larger for 5+ than for under-5s?
=====================================================================================

Short answer
------------
It comes down to two age-dependent immunity effects compounding each other in
the asymptomatic (A) compartment specifically: over-5s carry a much bigger
asymptomatic reservoir, and LM is far worse at detecting it in that age group.
HRP2 counts every A/D/Tr infection unconditionally, but LM only counts A
infections with a probability that collapses as acquired immunity builds up
with age/exposure - so the "invisible to LM but HRP2 positive" pool is much
larger in the 5+ group.

The two effects, quantified
-----------------------------
I inspected the equilibrium-initialised population directly (human_population
= 5000, init_EIR = 10 - the same setup as scratch_hrp2.R) to isolate this from
any timestep-to-timestep noise:

  Fraction of each age group in state A (asymptomatic):
    under-5: 30.9%        5+: 48.0%
  Fraction of each age group in state D/Tr (clinical):
    under-5: 1.26%        5+: 0.75%

  Mean id (acquired immunity to LM detectability):
    under-5: 3.0          5+: 20.3   (~6.7x higher in 5+)
  Mean ica (acquired clinical immunity):
    under-5: 3.5          5+: 39.4   (~11x higher in 5+)

  Mean LM detection probability among asymptomatic (A) individuals
  (probability_of_detection(), R/human_infection.R:1056):
    under-5: 99.0%         5+: 60.4%

Why this happens (the code)
------------------------------
1. clinical_immunity() (R/human_infection.R:1024) computes phi, the
   probability a new infection becomes clinical (D/Tr) rather than
   asymptomatic (A), as a decreasing function of acquired clinical immunity
   (ica). Older/more-exposed individuals have accumulated far more ica (via
   boost_immunity() on every infection, decaying slowly at rate rc), so phi
   is lower for them - more of their new infections resolve as A rather than
   D/Tr. This is the well-known "clinical immunity is acquired faster than
   anti-parasite immunity" pattern from the underlying Winskill 2017 model,
   and it's why the A fraction is ~1.5x larger in the 5+ group above.

2. probability_of_detection() (R/human_infection.R:1056) computes q, the
   probability an asymptomatic (A) infection is picked up by LM, as a
   decreasing function of both age and acquired detection immunity (id):
   `d1 + (1 - d1) / (1 + fd * (id / id0)^kd)`, where `fd` itself increases
   towards 1 with age. So detection probability is pushed down by TWO
   age-correlated forces at once - the age term directly, and the higher id
   that older/more-exposed individuals have accumulated (also via
   boost_immunity(), decaying at rate rid). This is why mean LM detection
   probability among A individuals falls from 99% (under-5) to 60% (5+).

3. D and Tr are always 100% LM-detectable (create_prevalence_renderer(),
   R/render.R: `clinically_detected <- state$get_index_of(c('Tr','D'))`,
   unconditional) and always trigger HRP2 unconditionally
   (update_hrp2_and_render_infections(), R/hrp2.R). So the D/Tr compartment
   never contributes to the HRP2/LM gap in either age group - the entire gap
   is generated in the A compartment, where LM applies a probability
   discount and HRP2 does not.

Putting it together
---------------------
Roughly, the "LM-invisible but HRP2-positive" share of each age group is
(fraction in A) * (1 - mean detection probability among A):
  under-5: 30.9% * (1 - 0.99)  ~= 0.3 percentage points
  5+:      48.0% * (1 - 0.604) ~= 19   percentage points

That lines up with the direction and rough scale of what scratch_hrp2.R
actually shows (5+ delta ~13pp, under-5 delta ~1pp in the simulated run) -
the two age-dependent immunity effects (bigger A reservoir + much lower LM
detectability within it) both push the same way for 5+, and both are close
to negligible for under-5s, which is why the delta is so lopsided between
the two age groups.

Comparing the infection recovery rate to the HRP2 clearance rate
=====================================================================================

Using the scratch script's parameters at the time (hrp2_scale = 50, matching
what was then hrp2_shape = 1, i.e. exponential) against the default
disease-state durations:

  State/process                Mean duration     Rate (per day)
  D  (clinical, untreated)     5 days            0.200
  Tr (treated)                 5 days            0.200
  U  (subpatent)                110 days          0.0091
  A  (asymptomatic)            195 days          0.0051
  HRP2 persistence             50 days           0.0198 (constant/exponential)

A few things stand out:

- HRP2 clears ~10x slower than the clinical episode itself (50 days vs 5
  days for D/Tr) - consistent with the real-world biology of HRP2 outlasting
  the acute illness.

- HRP2 clears ~4x faster than the asymptomatic (A) state itself (50 days vs
  195 days). Since most HRP2-positive individuals now come from A infections
  (per the asymptomatic-proportion analysis above), this means a person can
  lose HRP2 positivity while still asymptomatically infected - HRP2
  clearance in this model is a fixed hazard from the moment of infection,
  completely decoupled from whether the person has actually cleared
  parasites. That's a modelling simplification worth being aware of: it's
  not "HRP2 clears once parasites clear," it's "HRP2 clears on a fixed
  clock regardless of ongoing infection status" (unless reinfected, which
  restarts the clock via update_hrp2_and_render_infections(), R/hrp2.R).

- The hazard is constant (memoryless/exponential): a newly-infected person
  is exactly as likely to clear HRP2 tomorrow as someone infected 40 days
  ago. This was originally implemented as a weibull survival function
  (shape/scale), which allows a "low-then-rising" hazard shape when
  shape > 1 - but that's inconsistent with how every other infection state
  in the model (D/A/U/Tr) is cleared, which is always a constant hazard of
  1/duration. HRP2 clearance has since been switched to that same
  exponential/constant-hazard convention (see clear_hrp2_group(), R/hrp2.R),
  and hrp2_shape/hrp2_shape_treated no longer exist as parameters -
  hrp2_scale/hrp2_scale_treated are now directly the mean duration of HRP2
  positivity, matching dd/da/du/dt.
